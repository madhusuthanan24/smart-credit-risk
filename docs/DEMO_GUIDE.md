### Step 2: Running a Credit Risk Assessment (Minutes 1:30 – 3:30)

#### Actions:
1. Click **New Assessment** in the left sidebar.
2. Select a pre-configured applicant profile or enter sample attributes:
   - **Checking Account**: `A14` (No checking account)
   - **Duration**: `12 Months`
   - **Credit Amount**: `1,500 DM`
   - **Savings**: `A65` (Unknown / Substantial)
   - **Housing**: `A152` (Owns Home)
3. Click **Submit Credit Assessment**.
4. Observe the results card that appears instantly.

#### What to Say:
> *"Here, the credit officer enters the applicant's financial attributes across 20 standard origination dimensions. When we submit, our local ML engine—a tuned Logistic Regression model—executes the inference pipeline.*
> 
> *Notice three critical metrics:
> 1. Default Probability: 2.86%.
> 2. Decision Cutoff Threshold: 0.35. We do not use a naive 50% cutoff; extensive economic optimization showed that 0.35 captures 76.7% of defaulters, cutting potential loss by 37.6%.
> 3. Risk Category: 'LOW RISK' with an automated recommendation of 'GOOD CREDIT / APPROVED'."*

### Step 4: Interactive What-If Simulator (Minutes 5:00 – 7:00)

#### Actions:
1. Click **Risk Simulator** in the sidebar.
2. Show the baseline applicant attributes on the left.
3. On the right, modify features to simulate higher financial stress:
   - Increase **Duration** from `12` to `48 Months`.
   - Increase **Credit Amount** from `1,500` to `9,000 DM`.
   - Change **Checking Account** to `A11` (`< 0 DM Deficit`).
4. Click **Run Risk Simulation**.
5. Inspect the side-by-side comparison cards and the **Probability Delta** ($\Delta P$).

#### What to Say:
> *"In real underwriting, officers often want to see what happens if loan terms change. Our Risk Simulator allows officers to test counterfactual scenarios in real time.
> 
> Notice how increasing duration to 48 months and amount to 9,000 causes the default probability to jump from 2.86% to over 40%, crossing the 0.35 cutoff into 'HIGH RISK'.
> 
> Crucially, this simulation executes strictly in memory. It never pollutes or overwrites production records in our database."*
